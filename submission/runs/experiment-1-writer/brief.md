# Sarvottam Cables Ltd (SRVCABLE) — research brief

As of 2026-09-23

## Snapshot

Sarvottam Cables Ltd manufactures power cables, wires, and conductors. For Q1 FY27, official reporting shows revenue from operations rose 18.0% year over year to 1,248 crore rupees, although a secondary news source reported 1,428 crore rupees (35% growth); the company official press release is the preferred source. [S01](#source-s01) [S03](#source-s03)
Operating performance for Q1 FY27 saw EBITDA reach 140 crore rupees, up 2.6% year over year, with EBITDA margin contracting 170 basis points to 11.2%, while profit after tax rose 5.9% year over year to 82 crore rupees. [S03](#source-s03)
The company order book stood at 3,900 crore rupees as of 30 June 2026, an increase from 3,150 crore rupees recorded a year earlier. [S03](#source-s03)

## Bull case

- Geographic diversification expanded as exports rose to 21% of revenue in Q1 FY27 from 16% in Q1 FY26, alongside domestic niche growth where management projects data-centre cables, currently 9% of domestic sales, could double in two years. [S02](#source-s02) [S03](#source-s03)
- Capacity is scheduled to expand by roughly 30% through the new Bharuch plant slated for commercial production in Q3 FY27, supporting management full-year FY27 revenue growth guidance of 15% to 17%. [S02](#source-s02) [S03](#source-s03)
- Balance sheet leverage remains contained with net debt to equity at 0.35 times, funding 70% of the 620 crore rupee Bharuch capex primarily via internal accruals alongside term debt, while promoter pledged shares declined to 4.1% of promoter holdings as of 30 June 2026. [S02](#source-s02) [S08](#source-s08)

## Bear case

- Working capital stretched as receivable days lengthened from 78 to 96 days over the past year, driven by slower collections from two large state utility customers. [S02](#source-s02)
- Operating profitability faces input cost pressure as higher copper prices compressed Q1 FY27 EBITDA margins by 170 basis points, with price-variation clauses on two-thirds of contracts subject to a one-to-two-quarter lag before costs can be passed on. [S02](#source-s02) [S03](#source-s03)
- Subsequent liabilities pose balance sheet exposure following a 46.3 crore rupee GST demand order covering input tax credit disputes from FY2020-21 and FY2021-22, which represents a contingent cash outflow notwithstanding management intention to appeal. [S07](#source-s07)

## Open questions

- Why did secondary reporting present Q1 FY27 revenue at 1,428 crore rupees instead of the officially reported 1,248 crore rupees? [S01](#source-s01) [S03](#source-s03)
- When will payment collections from the two state utility customers normalize, and will prolonged receivable cycles require higher working capital debt? [S02](#source-s02)
- Will the planned appeal against the 46.3 crore rupee GST demand succeed, or could an adverse tax adjudication affect cash reserves during the final phase of Bharuch capex? [S02](#source-s02) [S07](#source-s07)
