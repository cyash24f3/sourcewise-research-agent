## How did you build it?

## Which model(s) did you use, and why those?

I used Gemini 3.8 Flash through Google's official Python SDK and Interactions API.
I chose it for the free-tier workflow, document context, speed, and structured JSON
output. Both stages use medium reasoning: high reasoning spent most of its output
budget thinking and sometimes returned incomplete JSON. The first call assesses
sources and extracts evidence; the second writes the brief. Python validates the
evidence and creates the source links. The program does not enable billing or
guarantee continued free quota.

## Design decision

I separate evidence extraction from writing. The first stage classifies every
source and records facts with source excerpts and conflicting figures. Local
checks reject unknown sources, unsupported numbers, and unmatched quotations.
The writer references evidence IDs, and Python inserts links from original
metadata. Small quote-copy errors can be aligned to a unique original paragraph,
with the original response and every change preserved. This keeps suspicious
document instructions and conflicting secondary figures from silently shaping
the final brief.

## Limitation

A matching quotation proves where text came from, not whether the claim is true
or correctly interpreted. Review caught errors that passed structural checks,
including confusing capex spending with funding and treating a lower margin as
lower absolute profit. I retained those failures and refined the writer prompt.
The agent has no live market feed or valuation model.

## Test log

https://github.com/cyash24f3/sourcewise-research-agent/blob/main/TEST_LOG.md

I ran three real research experiments with changed prompts/designs. The first two
extractions stopped on quote-copy errors and were resumed after an auditable
deterministic alignment step, then each made a real writer call. The third was a
fresh two-stage run shown in the demo. Reviews exposed overly dense writing, an
omitted business description, unsupported adjectives, and imprecise margin wording.
A final additional writer_v4 call used a complete reviewed ledger and produced the
314-word submission brief. All raw outputs, receipts, prompt snapshots and review
notes are in the repository. The offline suite has 110 passing tests.

## Snapshot

Sarvottam Cables Ltd manufactures power cables, wires, and conductors. [S03](#source-s03)
In Q1 FY27 ended 30 June 2026, revenue rose 18.0% year over year to 1,248 crore rupees, and EBITDA rose 2.6% to 140 crore rupees. [S03](#source-s03)

## Bull case

- The order book reached 3,900 crore rupees by 30 June 2026, while exports contributed 21% of quarterly revenue and data-centre cables provided 9% of domestic sales, which management expects could double in two years. [S02](#source-s02) [S03](#source-s03)
- The planned Bharuch plant is expected to add about 30% cable capacity in Q3 FY27, with about 70% of its 620 crore rupee budget spent, modest net debt to equity of 0.35 times, and maintained annual revenue growth guidance of 15% to 17%. [S02](#source-s02) [S03](#source-s03)
- Pledged promoter shares dropped to 4.1% of the 58.2% promoter holding as of 30 June 2026, down from 11.5% a year prior. [S04](#source-s04) [S08](#source-s08)

## Bear case

- EBITDA margin narrowed by 170 basis points following a nearly 14% rise in copper costs; while management expects to pass through higher prices, about two-thirds of contracts have price-variation clauses resetting with a one-to-two-quarter lag. [S01](#source-s01) [S02](#source-s02) [S03](#source-s03)
- Time taken to collect payments rose from 78 to 96 days over the past year due to delays from two state utility clients, though management affirmed no defaults have occurred. [S02](#source-s02)
- On 2 September 2026, the company received a 46.3 crore rupee GST demand order; while management intends to appeal and expects no material impact, the liability remains pending. [S07](#source-s07)

- How quickly can management normalize cash collections from the two state utility customers to reduce payment collection times from 96 days? [S02](#source-s02)
